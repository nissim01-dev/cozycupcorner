Buying guide: what to look for in a mug warmer

Flat-bottomed mugs work best

Plate warmers transfer heat through contact, so a mug with a flat, thin base (ceramic, glass or metal) heats far better than one with a raised ring or a thick, uneven bottom. Double-walled insulated tumblers block the heat.

Temperature settings

Basic warmers have one setting; better ones offer three or more. If you like your coffee genuinely hot, look for a top setting around 150°F or above.

Auto shut-off

For peace of mind in a home office, choose a warmer with an automatic shut-off or timer.

Cover your mug

A simple lid or saucer on top dramatically reduces heat loss — the easiest trick to make any warmer work better.

Plate warmer or smart mug?

Plate warmers are affordable and work with mugs you already love. A smart mug like the Ember keeps an exact temperature and can travel around the house on battery, but it costs considerably more.

FAQ

Can a mug warmer reheat cold coffee?

Most plate warmers are designed to keep hot drinks warm, not to reheat cold ones. Start with hot coffee.

Are mug warmers safe to leave on?

Choose one with auto shut-off or a timer, keep it on a stable surface, and don't leave it on unattended for long periods.
