Our rule of thumb: if you make one milk drink a day and don't mind heating milk yourself, a handheld wand is plenty. If you want a hot latte with zero effort — or you make drinks for two or more people — an electric frother is worth the counter space.

How to make cold foam at home

Cold foam is simply cold milk frothed until thick, then spooned on top of iced coffee or cold brew. Here's a reliable method:

  1. Start with very cold milk. Skim or 2% milk foams tallest; a mix of heavy cream and 2% gives the rich, sweet-cream style.
  2. Add a teaspoon of syrup or a little vanilla before frothing (it foams better than stirring in later).
  3. Froth with a handheld wand for 20–40 seconds, keeping the whisk just under the surface, or use the cold setting on an electric frother.
  4. Pour slowly over iced coffee so the foam floats.

Trend to try: blend a spoonful of marshmallow fluff into the milk before frothing for "marshmallow cold foam" — a fast-rising Pinterest search this year.

Buying guide: choosing a milk frother

What milk will you use?

Dairy milk foams most predictably. Oat "barista" blends foam very well; almond and some other plant milks produce lighter, shorter-lived foam. If you mostly use plant milk, an electric frother with a dense-foam setting gives the best results.

Hot drinks, cold drinks or both?

For iced lattes and cold foam, any handheld wand works; for hot lattes without extra steps, choose an electric model that heats. Many electric models (like the SIMPLETASTE and Aeroccino 3) do both.

Capacity

Electric frothers list two numbers: how much milk they can heat and how much they can froth (always less, because foam expands). If you make two lattes at once, check the froth capacity.

Cleaning

Non-stick interiors wipe clean easily; clean right after use so milk doesn't dry on. For handheld wands, run the whisk in warm soapy water for a few seconds. Avoid submerging any base that contains electronics.

Power source

Battery wands are cheapest; rechargeable (USB-C) wands are stronger and don't need battery swaps; electric jugs need an outlet near your coffee station.

Setting up a latte corner? Pair a frother with a mug warmer and some glass iced-coffee cups for the full home-café look.

FAQ

Can a handheld frother make cold foam?

Yes. Froth cold milk (skim or a cream-and-milk mix works best) for 20–40 seconds. Rechargeable wands with stronger motors make thicker foam faster.

Do I need an espresso machine to use a milk frother?

No. Frothed milk works with strong brewed coffee, AeroPress, moka pot, instant espresso, cold brew concentrate, matcha or chai.

Handheld or electric — which lasts longer?

It depends on the model and how you care for it. Electric frothers do more (heating, consistent foam) and you should avoid submerging the base; handheld wands are simpler and cheap to replace.

Why won't my plant milk foam?

Many plant milks lack the proteins that stabilize foam. Look for 'barista' versions, which are formulated to froth, and make sure the milk is fresh and cold.
