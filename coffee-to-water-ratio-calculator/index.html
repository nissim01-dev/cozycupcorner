How to brew it

    Tablespoons are approximate (about 5 grams of ground coffee per level tablespoon). Grind size and roast change how much fits in a spoon, so a scale is more consistent.

    Why the ratio matters more than the scoop

    Coffee tastes right when the water pulls out just enough flavor from the grounds. Too little coffee for the water and the cup tastes thin and sour; too much and it tastes heavy and bitter. A ratio such as 1:16 just means 1 gram of coffee for every 16 grams (or ml) of water, so it scales from one mug to a full carafe.

    Scoops and "cups" vary a lot. A coffee maker's "cup" is usually 5 oz, a mug is often 8 to 12 oz, and a heaped scoop can hold almost twice a level one. Weighing once with a kitchen scale and remembering the number is the easiest upgrade most home brewers can make.

    FAQ

    How much coffee do I use for 12 cups?

    A 12-cup drip coffee maker counts 5 oz (about 148 ml) cups, so 12 cups is roughly 1,775 ml of water. At a 1:16 ratio that is about 111 grams of ground coffee, or around 21 level tablespoons. Use a little less for a milder pot.

    What is the best coffee to water ratio for French press?

    Most brew guides land between 1:12 and 1:16 for French press, and 1:15 is a good starting point. For a 34 oz press filled to about 1,000 ml, that is about 67 grams of coarse coffee. Steep 4 minutes, then press slowly.

    How many tablespoons of coffee per cup?

    A level tablespoon holds about 5 grams of ground coffee. For one 8 oz mug (240 ml) at 1:16 you need about 15 grams, which is roughly 3 level tablespoons. A kitchen scale is more accurate because grind size changes how much fits in a spoon.
