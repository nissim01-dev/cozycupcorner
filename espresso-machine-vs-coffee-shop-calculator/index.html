How we do the math

  • Saved per drink = café price − home cost per drink.
  • Saved per month = drinks per week × saved per drink × 52 ÷ 12 (about 4.33 weeks).
  • Months to break even = machine cost ÷ saved per month.
  • Net saved in 5 years = saved per year × 5 − machine cost.

What the number doesn't show

The calculator assumes you replace every café drink with one made at home. Most people keep a café visit or two for the treat or the company, so try a lower drinks-per-week number to see a more realistic result. It also leaves out descaling supplies, replacement parts and the time it takes to make a drink, but it doesn't count the friends and family you'll end up making lattes for either.

The type of machine changes the home cost too. A semi-automatic with a grinder needs the most practice but has the lowest cost per drink, because you buy whole beans. A super-automatic grinds and brews at the press of a button. A pod machine is the easiest, but capsules usually cost more per drink than beans, so enter your pod cost as the home cost.

FAQ

Is an espresso machine worth it?

It usually is if you buy café drinks several times a week and would actually make them at home. Enter how many café drinks you buy, what you pay, what the machine would cost you and your home cost per drink. If the break-even is under a year or two, the machine tends to pay for itself.

How long does it take an espresso machine to pay for itself?

Divide the machine cost by what you save each month. Monthly savings are your café drinks per week, times the difference between the café price and your home cost per drink, times about 4.33 weeks. Heavy café drinkers often break even within months; occasional drinkers can take years.

How do I work out my home cost per drink?

Divide what you paid for a bag of beans by the number of shots it makes. A double shot uses about 18 grams, so a 12 oz (340 g) bag makes about 18 doubles. Add the milk, syrup or pods you use, and you have your home cost per drink.
